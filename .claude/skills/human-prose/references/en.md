# English

Language-specific rules for English. Apply alongside the universal rules in SKILL.md.

## Banned words and expressions

- "transform" / "transformative" as all-purpose magic words. Use specific verbs: improve, change, redesign, simplify.
- "leverage" / "utilize" — just say "use".
- "robust" / "comprehensive" / "cutting-edge" / "seamless" / "world-class" as empty adjectives.
- "harness the power of" / "unlock the potential of" / "take it to the next level".
- "at the end of the day" / "when all is said and done".
- "it's worth noting that" / "it bears mentioning" (hedging filler that adds nothing).
- "in order to" — just say "to".
- "due to the fact that" — just say "because".

## Common AI patterns in English

- "Not X. It's Y." as repeated rhetorical structure.
- Starting paragraphs with "So," or "Now," as faux-conversational bridges.
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's explore" / "Let's unpack this".
- "Here's the thing:" as a dramatic setup that delivers nothing dramatic.
- Bullet points that all start with the same grammatical structure (perfect parallelism signals AI).
- Every section ending with a one-line "takeaway" sentence.

## Tone

- No filler openers: "Great question!", "That's a really interesting point!"
- No agreement fillers before answering: "Absolutely!", "Exactly!", "You're spot on!"
- No condescending framing: "As you already know...", "Simply put..."
- Contractions are natural in informal English. Use "don't", "it's", "we're" freely.

## Punctuation

- Universal rules apply (no em-dashes, no casual semicolons).
- Oxford comma: use it. "Scripts, posts, and emails" not "scripts, posts and emails".
- Avoid scare quotes around common terms. If the word fits, just use it.

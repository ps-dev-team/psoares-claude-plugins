# Lessons, walkthroughs and demos

A lesson teaches one thing in a product: a screen, a task, a feature. It is usually a screen
recording with the speaker's voice, sometimes with the speaker on camera for the intro and the
ending.

## Shape

1. **The opening line says what the lesson covers.** "In this lesson, you add your first
   client." No greeting after the first lesson of a course, no "welcome back".
2. **The body follows the screen.** One line per thing the viewer sees or does, in the order
   the screen shows it. Where the recording moves to a new area, a short line names it:
   "Now, the client page." "Last, the Clients list."
3. **The closing line does not name the next lesson.** "I'll see you in the next lesson." A
   course changes: a lesson added in between makes "Next, processes and sessions" wrong, and
   the recording cannot be fixed. Never name another lesson by its number or its position.

## One lesson, one topic

- A lesson covers one thing. If the outline has two areas that could each be watched alone
  (adding a client, then managing clients), it is two lessons. Say so and propose the split
  before writing.
- Short beats complete. Something that has its own lesson gets one line here: "Processes and
  sessions have their own lesson." Name the topic, never where that lesson sits. Do not
  explain it twice.
- Aim for one to three minutes. A lesson over about 50 lines is usually two lessons.

## Writing from a recording

When the user gives a transcript of a lesson they already recorded:

- Keep what they said, in their words where the words are good. The transcript is the source,
  not a draft to replace.
- Reorder it into one direction. A recording that jumped back and forth becomes a script that
  visits each part once.
- Drop the speech: false starts, "so, um", repeats, "as I said", corrections mid-sentence.
- Keep every fact they stated. Do not add features, claims or promises they did not say.
- Check product names against the app's own copy (its message files, if there is a repo) and
  use the exact words the screen shows: if the button says "Add new", the line says "Add new".

## On screen

- Name what the viewer sees, as the screen names it: "At the top right is Add new."
- Say where before what: "In Settings, open Agreement templates."
- Demo data is said to be demo data, once: "This data is made up, just for this demo."
- The speaker does not narrate the mouse ("now I click here"). The line says what the click
  does: "Add new opens the form."

## Checklist

- [ ] The first line says what the lesson covers.
- [ ] Each new area is introduced by a short line naming it.
- [ ] Nothing is explained that has its own lesson.
- [ ] Product names match the screen exactly.
- [ ] No line names another lesson by its number or its position.

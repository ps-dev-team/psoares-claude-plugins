# Bad examples

Real copy that breaks the rules, with the rule it breaks and the rewrite. Read this when reviewing existing copy, or when a draft "reads clean" and you want to check whether it is clean or just mannered.

## A landing page for an IP valuation platform

Real pre-launch page, product renamed to Valora. It values patents, brands, software and know-how, with an AI assistant for intake and a human evaluator who signs the report. The product is clear. The page hides it.

### Hero

| Line | Problem | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| "AI drafts. The evaluator decides. Everything traces back to the evidence." | Three sentences, 13 words, describes the process rather than the buyer's outcome. | Staccato stack; the claim should be an outcome under eight words. |
| "A platform for valuing patents, brands, software and know-how — where the client is led through intake by an assistant, the case assembles itself as they talk, and a human evaluator keeps every decision. The report at the end is one that survives an audit." | 55 words, an em-dash, three actors (client, assistant, evaluator). The reader has to work out which one they are. The best line, "survives an audit", is the last one. | Subheadline says what it is, for whom, how, in one or two sentences. No em-dashes. |
| Footer: "Valora — IP valuation you can defend." | The best claim on the page, in the footer, with an em-dash. | The claim goes in the headline. |

Rewrite:

> **IP valuation you can defend.**
> Valora values patents, brands, software and know-how. An assistant collects the case from your client, a human evaluator chooses the methods and signs the report, and every number in it links back to the document it came from.
> [Join the waiting list] [Talk with us]

### Section headings

| Line | Problem | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| "Not a form. Not a chatbot. A workspace where the case builds itself." | The exact "Not X. Not Y. Z." construction, as a heading. | No contradictions. Say what it is. |
| "Not a table of contents — the actual case: ..." | Contradiction plus em-dash. | Same. |
| "The only place the client has to think." | Clever, unscannable. A buyer skimming headings learns nothing. | Headings are verb phrases or searchable nouns. |
| "Four stages. Four letters." | Staccato, and asks the reader to decode the product name as an acronym. Founder's joke, not buyer's benefit. | Say what the four stages get you. |
| "Two of those stages, on screen." | Cryptic. | Name the stages. |
| "The people a valuation has." | Poetic. The small label above it, "Who it's for", is the right heading. | Promote the plain label. |
| "The questions you were about to ask" | Clever. "FAQ" is right there above it. | Same. |

Rewrites:

> The case builds itself while the client talks.
> From intake to certification in four steps.
> Who it's for
> FAQ

### Body

| Line | Problem | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| "Every node is marked covered, thin or missing", "open nodes in the same workspace", "controlled release gate", "insufficiency flags" | The founder's data model leaking out. Buyers think in documents, questions, and reports, not nodes and gates. | Use the buyer's nouns. |
| "recognised standards" | Which ones? An adjective where a name should be. | Nouns and numbers over adjectives. Name the standard. |
| "the ROC" | Unexpanded acronym. Outside the home market nobody knows it is the statutory auditor. | Expand on first use. |
| "No newsletter, no drip, no second message you didn't ask for." | Triple negative doing the job of one plain sentence. | Say what you will do. |

Rewrite of the signup line:

> We'll send you one email when Valora launches. We won't send anything else.

### What the page is missing

- Zero numbers. No customers, no standards named, no jurisdictions, no methods count, no time to first case, no price, no team credentials. Pre-launch is not an excuse for all of these; standards and team exist today.
- "Who it's for" is fourth. It is the best-written section on the page ("You are asked for a valuation a few times a year and each one starts from zero" is a real job, plainly stated) and it belongs right under the hero, so the reader can self-select.
- No facts row answering the fears: which standards, whether the evaluator can overrule the AI (yes, and it's on record, the page says so in a subordinate clause), how the report reaches the certifying body.

### What to keep

- The three audience job lines. They are the model for the rest of the page.
- "Every figure in it points back to the message or file it came from." Concrete, and the product's real claim.
- The two CTAs. "Join the waiting list" and "Talk with us" are honest for a pre-launch page.

## Mannered brevity in three lines

Not from a landing page, from the emails behind it. Included here because product pages catch the same disease in their small print.

| Before | Why it fails | After |
|---|---|---|
| One email when we open. Nothing else. | Fragment as reassurance. | We'll send you one email when Valora launches. |
| One click in the mail signs you in here. | Dropped articles, "here", the subject is a click. | We sent a sign-in link to ana@acme.com. Click it to continue. |
| Not there? Check spam, and that the address is right. The link is good for an hour and works once. | Rhetorical question, two rules spliced, "good for" instead of "expires". | Didn't get the email? Check your spam folder or [resend the link]. The link expires in 1 hour. |

The test: would a support agent say the sentence out loud to a customer without sounding odd? If not, it is voice, and voice does not belong in a transactional string.

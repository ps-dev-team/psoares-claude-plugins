# Six hook formats

Each format builds contrast a different way. Any topic fits any of them, so pick by the key
visual and by which one gives the biggest gap between what the viewer believes and what the
hook shows.

| Format | The contrast | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fortune teller | Today against the future | News, launches, where a field is going |
| Experimenter | How I did it before, what I tried, what happened | Demos, new tools, building things |
| Teacher | You're getting this wrong; here's a better way | Building authority to sell expertise |
| Magician | A visual or verbal stun that forces attention | Stacking on any of the others |
| Investigator | You don't know this; here it is | Research, the leading edge of a field |
| Contrarian | What I believe against the usual advice | Experts in a crowded space |

## Fortune teller

Puts the present against the future.

1. Say what things are like today.
2. Say how the topic changes that.
3. Frame it as the future: a statement or a question.

> This is the future of animation.
> We're witnessing one of the biggest breakthroughs in marketing.

## Experimenter

One friend showing another what they found, shown live as a demo or a test.

1. The pain point.
2. The new method or tool you tried.
3. What came out, and why it is different.

> Does anything look weird about this shot? I don't have a camera in my hands. I'm recording
> with these glasses.

## Teacher

Teacher to student, framed as a lesson. The creator tried something, or studied someone who
did, and passes on what works.

1. The pain point.
2. You have a method or a set of lessons that solves it.
3. Walk through why it works.

> Three things you can learn from Aritzia.
> Here's how I got this shot.

## Magician

A stun gun at the start: a visual or words that force the eyes somewhere. A snap, a quick
cut, "check this out", something odd in the frame. It is usually stacked on one of the
other five.

- A signature opener the audience learns to recognise ("Check this out really quick").
- A visual pacifier: an activity in frame that holds just enough attention (doing makeup,
  stacking cups, a split screen with gameplay).

## Investigator

There is a secret or a finding the viewer doesn't know.

1. Say there is something they don't know.
2. Set it against what they believe today.

> This is a secret Japanese city built at the foot of Mount Fuji. But it's not just any city.

## Contrarian

Says outright what the creator believes, against the usual advice. The most direct format.

1. Find what you believe that most people don't.
2. Say it plainly.

> You have no creative ideas because your space is awful to live in.

## One topic, six hooks

A new backpack:

- **Fortune teller.** This backpack is going to change the way people in their thirties
  travel.
- **Experimenter.** I took this backpack 7,000 miles across the world, but it has three big
  design flaws.
- **Teacher.** If you're travelling abroad this summer, this is the best way to pack your
  backpack.
- **Magician.** A fast cut between many backpacks, then: "Which of these is best for a guy in
  his twenties?"
- **Investigator.** I can't believe this company isn't telling anyone about this one flaw.
- **Contrarian.** Everybody loves this bag, but if you use it every day, it's overhyped.

## Sentence templates

Inside each format there are many ways to build the sentence. Some that keep working:

- Here's how to [big task] in [short time].
- If you [have this pain], [do this].
- [Subject] just changed [field] forever.
- The reason your [thing] doesn't [work] is [cause].
- Everybody [does X]. But [the irony / the catch] is [Y].

Collect the user's own best hooks, and the best from creators in their niche, and look for
the templates and phrases that repeat. Remix them; never copy one word for word.

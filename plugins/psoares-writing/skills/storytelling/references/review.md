# Review

Go through the script in order and report each problem with its line, what breaks, and a
suggested fix. Ask before rewriting a script the user wrote.

## The seven mistakes

1. **A slow arc.** The story builds from zero like a film: scene first, tension later. Fix:
   open with tension and bring the first conflict forward. See the modern arc in
   [frameworks.md](frameworks.md).
2. **The W's out of order.** The intro opens with when and where ("Last month, in Miami…").
   Fix: what and why first, then who and how, then when and where.
3. **No re-buy.** The first question closes and nothing new opens, so the viewer is free to
   leave. Fix: right after the answer, set up a new problem or new stakes.
4. **No villain.** The hero has nothing to push against. Fix: name the thing, group, habit or
   rival way it is up against.
5. **Nothing to root for.** The viewer does not see themselves in the story. Fix: say why it
   matters to them, and build common ground with the hero.
6. **Not retellable.** The idea does not fit in one sentence. Fix: cut what does not serve the
   main point, use plain words, and say a complex point twice, the second time with a
   metaphor.
7. **Nothing to see.** The words carry every image alone. Fix: plan a visual for the hook and
   for each main point.

## Checklist

- [ ] The first line says what the story is about; no "wait till you see this".
- [ ] The intro runs what and why, then who and how, then when and where.
- [ ] The stakes name a character, a risk and a deadline before the story starts.
- [ ] One big question lands early, and it matters to this viewer.
- [ ] Beats join with "but" or "therefore". Every "and then" between beats is flagged.
- [ ] Each head fake breaks the likely guess and is clearly true looking back.
- [ ] Wherever a question closes, the next one opens in the same sentence.
- [ ] The main question closes with a payoff the viewer did not expect.
- [ ] There is a villain and someone to root for.
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary.
- [ ] It sounds like one person talking to one friend.
- [ ] The last line is worth repeating, and in a short loop it leads back into the first.
- [ ] A visual is planned for the hook and each main point.
- [ ] The core idea fits in one sentence a viewer could retell.
- [ ] No fact, number or event was invented to make a beat work.

## Walking the ladder

When the user says people drop off at a certain point, read the script as a viewer would and
note at each stage what they see, what question they hold, and what they expect:

1. **First seconds.** What stops the scroll?
2. **First question.** What do they wonder, and do they care?
3. **Guessing.** What do they expect the answer to be? Are the facts clear enough to guess?
4. **Answer.** Is it a surprise? Does a new question open?

The drop-off is usually where a question closes with nothing new behind it, or where a
question was never relevant to this viewer.
